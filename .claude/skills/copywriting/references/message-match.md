# Message match: the ad decides what the page must say

The single biggest lever on paid traffic conversion, and the one most often missed. A page that converts
organic traffic can fail on paid traffic for one reason: it answers a different question than the ad asked.

## The rule

**The first screen must repeat the promise of the ad, in the same words, and take the next step.**
If the ad says "departamentos desde $2.290 UF en Ñuñoa" and the page opens with "Bienvenidos a nuestro
proyecto", the visitor has to re-find what they came for. That gap shows up as high CTR with low conversion
— and gets misdiagnosed as a bidding problem.

## What must match

| Element in the ad | What the page owes it |
|---|---|
| The core promise | Same claim, same words, above the fold |
| Price or offer | Identical figure and condition. Never "from $X" in the ad and a different figure on the page |
| The qualifier ("for whom") | The page confirms the visitor is in the right place within one sentence |
| The CTA verb | The button says the same action the ad promised |
| Visual | The product, unit or model shown in the ad appears on the page |

## One page per intent

Search intents that deserve different pages: category + location, brand, financing or payment, comparison,
and offer. Sending all of them to one generic page is what makes CPL look expensive when the traffic was fine.

## Lead-gen forms

- 3–5 fields. Each extra field costs 5–10% of conversion — but 1–2 qualifying questions raise lead quality
  enough to be worth it when the goal is qualified leads, not volume.
- Ask the qualifier that the sales team actually uses to disqualify (budget, timing, financing, location).
- Label buttons with what happens next: "Quiero cotizar", not "Enviar".
- Never promise a channel the client does not staff ("respuesta inmediata por WhatsApp" without WhatsApp).

## Ecommerce product pages

- The first screen answers: what it is, what it costs, when it arrives, and what happens if it fails.
- Objections in order of frequency: size and fit (fashion), skin type and results timeline (beauty),
  shipping cost and delivery time (retail), returns policy (all).
- Proof next to the price, not at the bottom of the page.

## Quick audit

- [ ] Does the first screen repeat the ad's promise in its own words?
- [ ] Are the price and offer identical to the ad?
- [ ] Can the visitor act without scrolling?
- [ ] Is the CTA verb the one the ad used?
- [ ] Does the page load fast enough on mobile that the promise arrives at all?
