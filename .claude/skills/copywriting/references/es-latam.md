# Spanish for LATAM: register and neutrality

## Register

Decide once per brand and never mix within a page:

| Register | When it fits | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Tú** | Consumer brands, beauty, fashion, DTC, younger audiences | Reads too casual for high-ticket or institutional |
| **Usted** | Finance, health, legal, corporate, older audiences, high-ticket real estate | Can read distant if the whole page uses it |
| **Vos** | Argentina, Uruguay, parts of Central America | Only when the brand's market is specifically there |
| **Ustedes** | Plural, everywhere in LATAM | "Vosotros" belongs to Spain only |

Chile, Mexico, Colombia and Peru all default to **ustedes** in plural. A page written for Spain and reused
in LATAM is immediately audible.

## Neutral Spanish, with exceptions

Neutral Spanish travels; local slang converts better in a single market. The rule of thumb: neutral for
the page, local for the ad. Words that shift meaning across markets — *coche/auto/carro*, *piso/departamento*,
*móvil/celular*, *ordenador/computador* — follow the client's market, per the brand sheet.

## Common mistakes

- **English structure in Spanish words**: "Nosotros ayudamos empresas a crecer" instead of "Ayudamos a las
  empresas a crecer". Spanish drops the pronoun and needs the "a".
- **Title Case**: Spanish does not capitalise every word. "Cotiza Tu Departamento Hoy" reads translated;
  "Cotiza tu departamento hoy" reads native.
- **Literal calques**: "aplicar" for *apply* (a job), "remover" for *remove*, "customizar", "accionable".
- **Currency and numbers**: thousands separator is a period and decimals a comma in most of LATAM
  ($1.250.000 · 15,05). Getting this wrong makes a report look foreign.
- **Gender-inclusive forms**: follow the brand sheet. Do not decide unilaterally on a client's behalf.

## CTAs that work in Spanish

Weak: *Enviar · Más información · Click aquí · Registrarse*
Strong: *Quiero cotizar · Ver disponibilidad · Agendar mi visita · Calcular mi dividendo · Comprar ahora*

Formula: verb in first person or imperative + what they get. First person ("Quiero…") tends to outperform
in lead gen because it states the visitor's intent rather than the company's request.
